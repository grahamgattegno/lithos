The timeline

Chapter by chapter — how Lithos went from an idea to a real company website.

💎 It started with gems

The big idea: a company that helps ANYONE become a geologist. I started building a catalog of gemstones — names, colors, hardness, where they come from. One gem at a time.

⚔️ The first games

A catalog is cool, but games make learning FUN. Gem battles, warriors, quizzes, secret cheat codes. The site stopped being a list and started being a place to play.

🚀 Publishing day WIN

With a grown-up's help, Lithos went LIVE on the real internet. Typing my own web address and watching my site load... best feeling ever.

💵 The first sale WIN

Someone I didn't make buy it... actually paid real money for something I built. That's the day Lithos became a real business.

🌙 Night Shift at the Gem Factory

I built a survive-the-night game: cameras, doors, power, and gem animatronics that get angrier as the night goes on. Then 5 whole nights, paychecks, and a champion bonus.

😬 The character problem STRUGGLE

Hard lesson: I wanted to use famous game characters — but you can't use someone else's characters on a website that takes real money. Even changed-up versions. That one stung.

✨ So I invented my OWN WIN

Instead of borrowing characters, I created TEN originals: Sunspire, Duskveil, Clatterclaw, Redline, Aurum — and the Deep Crew: Magnapull, Prismimic, Glacivore, Swarmshard, and Nullite. Nobody can ever take them from me.

📈 Lithos grows HUGE WIN

From 100 gems to 1,200+ specimens with real photos. Real partnerships with real jewelry companies. Memberships, classes, careers, donations that help real miners.

🕹️ The Arcade opens

All my games in one place: Night Shift, Gem Rush (match-3 combos!), and Gem Digger — with drills, bombs, and a heat system I designed so you can't just spam-click. Plus one secret game for code-hunters...

📖 ...and the story continues

Every new game, gem, and idea becomes a new chapter. This page never really ends.

The struggles 😤

The honest page. Building things is HARD sometimes — here's the stuff that went wrong (and what I did about it).

🥶 The day Cursor froze

My code editor totally locked up — frozen, broken, would not work. I learned the builder's secret weapon: force-quit, restart the whole computer, and if all else fails, reinstall. It came back.

⚖️ The copyright lesson

I really, REALLY wanted famous characters in my game. Finding out I couldn't use them on a money-making site felt unfair at first. But it pushed me to invent my own — and mine are cooler.

🗂️ Too many file versions

gemstones-1, gemstones-2... gemstones-8?! I kept opening OLD copies and wondering where my new stuff went. Lesson: sort your Downloads by date and delete old copies!

🤪 The octillion incident

I typed a fake octillion dollars into my own money tracker for fun. Then I learned: an octillion is more money than exists on Earth — and real numbers, even small ones, beat pretend ones every time.

⏳ Everything takes longer than you think

"Quick" features took days. Big features took... more days. That's normal. Builders everywhere know this feeling.

📸 Fighting with screenshots

Even taking a picture of my screen turned into a whole adventure (Shift + ⌘ + 4, if you're wondering). Sometimes the small stuff is the annoying stuff.

Lessons for other kid builders 🧠

If you want to build your own website or company, here's what I learned the real way.

  1. Start small, then grow. Lithos began with a handful of gems. Now it has 1,200+. You don't need everything on day one.
  2. Make your OWN characters. Borrowing famous ones feels easier — but on a real business, it's not allowed. And inventing your own is more fun anyway.
  3. Real money = real rules. The moment your site takes real money, be extra honest: clear prices, no tricks, and a grown-up double-checking payments.
  4. Test everything. Every button, every game, every night. Bugs hide where you don't look.
  5. Struggles are chapters, not endings. Frozen apps, hard lessons, lost files — every builder has them. Keep going.
  6. Ask for help. Grown-ups for publishing and payments, tools for building, friends for feedback. Builders don't build alone.
  7. Keep a story like this one. Future-you will love reading how it all happened.
